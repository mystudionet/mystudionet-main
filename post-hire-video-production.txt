Most business owners don't know they hired the wrong production company until they're watching the rough cut. By then, they're weeks into a project, halfway through the budget, and stuck with footage that doesn't match what they had in mind.

The problem usually isn't the quality of the work. It's a mismatch: the wrong style, the wrong communication rhythm, the wrong scope. This checklist gives you 10 questions to ask before you sign anything.

## 1. Does Their Portfolio Match the Work You Need?

Reel quality is easy to fake. Any company can put together a highlight reel of their best moments across five years and a dozen different budgets. What you want to see is work similar to yours.

If you're a restaurant owner on Long Island looking for a 60-second promo, ask specifically for restaurant or food-service work. If you're a law firm needing a brand video, look for testimonial and interview-driven content. The gap between "we've done it before" and "we've done this exact thing before" matters.

## 2. Who Actually Does the Work?

Some companies pitch with senior talent and deliver with juniors or subcontractors. Ask directly: who will be on set? Who will edit the footage? Will the person you're meeting today be present on shoot day?

This isn't about distrust. It's about alignment. A team that has worked together before moves faster, communicates better, and delivers fewer surprises.

## 3. What's Included in the Quote?

The base quote rarely tells the whole story. Common add-ons that can inflate a budget: talent and casting fees, location permits, music licensing, color grading, motion graphics, and revision rounds beyond the first.

Ask for an itemized breakdown. A transparent company will provide one without hesitation. If the quote is a single number with no line items, push back.

## 4. How Many Revision Rounds Are Included?

One round of revisions is not enough for most projects. Two is reasonable. Three gives you room to refine without feeling rushed.

Find out exactly what counts as a revision: a single note versus a restructured cut are very different requests. Get the scope in writing before the project starts.

## 5. What's Their Turnaround Time?

Ask for a realistic timeline from shoot day to final delivery. Then ask what happens if that timeline slips. Production companies often have multiple projects running simultaneously, and delays cascade.

## 6. Do They Handle Scripting and Creative Direction?

Some businesses come with a clear script and just need an execution team. Others need a creative partner from the start. Know which category you fall into and confirm the company can meet you there.

If you need scripting, storyboarding, or shot planning, ask to see examples of their pre-production work, not just the finished product.

## 7. Have They Worked in Your Industry?

Industry familiarity matters more than it seems. A team that understands the pacing of a restaurant environment, the compliance constraints of a healthcare brand, or the tone of a B2B sales video will ask better questions in pre-production and make smarter decisions on set.

Ask: have you worked with businesses in my industry before? And: what did you learn from that project that made the next one better?

## 8. What Gear and Crew Size Do They Bring?

Gear isn't everything, but it tells you about the company's investment in their own craft. A two-person crew with a mirrorless camera can deliver excellent work for an interview-style video. A large-scale commercial might warrant a cinema camera, a gaffer, a sound mixer, and a dedicated director.

Match the crew size to the scope. Overstaffed shoots burn budget. Understaffed shoots cut corners on the day.

## 9. Do You Have a Contract?

Always work with a contract. It should cover: deliverables, timeline, payment schedule, usage rights, revision rounds, and who owns the footage after delivery. Footage ownership is a point many clients overlook. Make sure you're not paying for a video only to find out you can't repurpose it later.

## 10. What Happens if You're Not Happy with the Result?

Ask this directly and watch how they respond. A company with a clear quality assurance process will answer without hesitation. A company that hedges or deflects is telling you something.

Good production companies build delivery milestones into the contract so that dissatisfaction at the end is rare. By the time you're at final delivery, you should have already seen and approved a rough cut and an intermediate version.
