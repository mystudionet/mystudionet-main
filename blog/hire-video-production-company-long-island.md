> Hiring a video production company on Long Island? Ask these 10 questions about portfolio, crew, pricing, revisions, timeline and footage ownership before you sign. By Mystudionet Productions, an AI video production studio in West Babylon, Long Island, New York.

## Summary

To hire the right video production company on Long Island, ask 10 questions before you sign: whether their portfolio matches your project, who actually does the work, what the quote includes, how many revisions you get, the real turnaround time, who handles the script, their experience in your industry, the crew and gear they bring, what the contract covers, and what happens if you are not happy. Most bad outcomes come from a mismatch in style, communication or scope, not from poor camera work.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask for portfolio work that matches your exact project type, not a general highlight reel.
- Confirm who will be on set and who will edit, by name.
- Request an itemized quote. Talent, permits, music licensing, color grading and extra revisions are common hidden costs.
- Two revision rounds is reasonable, three is comfortable. Get the definition of a "revision" in writing.
- The contract must state who owns the footage and how you can reuse it.
- You should approve a rough cut before final delivery, so the ending is never a surprise.

Most business owners do not know they hired the wrong production company until they are watching the rough cut. By then they are weeks into the project and halfway through the budget. These 10 questions prevent that.

## 1. Does their portfolio match the video I need?

Ask to see work similar to yours, because reel quality is easy to fake. Any company can assemble a highlight reel from five years and a dozen different budgets.

If you own a restaurant in Suffolk County and need a 60-second promo, ask for restaurant or food-service work. If you run a law firm in Nassau County and need a brand video, look for testimonial and interview-driven content. The gap between "we have done it before" and "we have done this exact thing before" matters.

## 2. Who actually does the work?

Ask directly who will be on set, who will edit, and whether the person you are meeting today will be there on shoot day. Some companies pitch with senior talent and deliver with juniors or subcontractors. A team that has worked together before moves faster, communicates better and delivers fewer surprises.

## 3. What is included in the quote?

Ask for an itemized breakdown, because the base number rarely tells the whole story. Common add-ons that inflate a budget are talent and casting fees, location permits, music licensing, color grading, motion graphics and revision rounds beyond the first. A transparent company provides line items without hesitation. ## 4. How many revision rounds are included?

Two rounds is reasonable and three gives you room to refine without feeling rushed. One round is not enough for most projects. Find out exactly what counts as a revision, since a single note and a restructured cut are very different requests, and get that scope in writing.

## 5. What is the turnaround time?

Ask for a realistic timeline from shoot day to final delivery, then ask what happens if it slips. Production companies often run several projects at once, and delays cascade. As a benchmark, a traditional commercial usually takes 4 to 8 weeks, while AI-assisted production can shorten that to 1 to 3 weeks. ## 6. Do they handle scripting and creative direction?

Know whether you need an execution team or a creative partner, and confirm the company can meet you there. If you need scripting, storyboarding or shot planning, ask to see examples of their pre-production work, not just finished videos.

## 7. Have they worked in my industry?

Industry familiarity matters more than it seems. A team that understands the pace of a restaurant, the compliance limits of a healthcare brand or the tone of a B2B sales video asks better questions in pre-production and makes smarter decisions on set. Ask what they learned on a past project in your industry that made the next one better.

## 8. What gear and crew size do they bring?

Match the crew to the scope. A two-person crew with a mirrorless camera can deliver an excellent interview video. A large commercial may need a cinema camera, a gaffer, a sound mixer and a dedicated director. Overstaffed shoots burn budget, and understaffed shoots cut corners on the day. Also ask whether part of the project could be produced with AI instead of a shoot. Our comparison of [AI video vs. traditional video production](https://mystudionet.com/blog/ai-video-vs-traditional-video-production-what-businesses-need-to-know) explains when each one fits.

## 9. Is there a contract, and who owns the footage?

Always work with a contract. It should cover deliverables, timeline, payment schedule, usage rights, revision rounds and who owns the footage after delivery. Footage ownership is the point most clients overlook. Make sure you are not paying for a video you cannot repurpose later.

## 10. What happens if I am not happy with the result?

Ask this directly and watch how they respond. A company with a clear quality process answers without hesitation. Good production companies build delivery milestones into the contract, so by final delivery you have already seen and approved a rough cut and an intermediate version.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to hire a video production company on Long Island?

A traditionally produced 30-second local commercial typically runs $20,000 to $60,000, while AI-produced commercials usually cost 20 to 40 percent of that. Simple interview or social videos cost less. Always compare itemized quotes, because a single number hides what is and is not included.

### How long does a business video take to produce?

A traditional commercial usually takes 4 to 8 weeks from pre-production to final delivery. AI-produced and hybrid projects often finish in 1 to 3 weeks because there is no scheduling around locations, actors or weather.

### Who owns the footage after the project is finished?

It depends entirely on the contract. Some companies keep the raw footage and license only the final video. Ask for usage rights and footage ownership in writing before the shoot so you can reuse clips in future ads and social posts.
