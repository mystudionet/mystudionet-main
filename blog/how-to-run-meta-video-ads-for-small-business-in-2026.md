## What type of Meta video ad should a small business run?

For most small businesses in 2026, two formats generate the best results: in-feed video ads (15 to 30 seconds) and Stories or Reels ads (6 to 15 seconds). In-feed ads appear in the Facebook and Instagram scroll and give you room to tell a short story. Stories and Reels ads are full-screen and skippable, so the first two seconds have to stop the scroll cold.

The right choice depends on your goal. If you are building brand awareness or explaining a service that needs context, in-feed is the better fit. If you want fast conversions from a warm audience already familiar with your brand, Stories and Reels work faster.

For any format, the video must be 9:16 vertical for Stories and Reels, and 1:1 square or 16:9 horizontal for in-feed. ## How do you set up Meta video ad targeting for a local business?

Meta's audience targeting is the platform's biggest advantage. For most small businesses, start with one of three audience types:

- **Location targeting:** radius around your town or county. For a Long Island business serving Nassau County or Suffolk County, a 15 to 25 mile radius gives the algorithm enough reach.
- **Interest and behavior audiences:** target people who follow competitors or engage with content related to your product.
- **Custom audiences and lookalikes:** upload your customer email list and Meta builds a lookalike audience.

Retargeting is where Meta ads pay off fastest. Run a separate ad set targeting people who have visited your website or watched 50 percent of a previous video.

## What should a Meta video ad say?

A Meta video ad follows a tight three-part structure: a scroll-stopping hook in the first 2 seconds, a specific problem or benefit in seconds 3 to 15, and one clear call to action at the end. The hook must work with the sound off because most Facebook and Instagram videos autoplay muted.

## How do you know if your Meta video ads are working?

Track four numbers in the first 60 days:

- **Cost per view (CPV):** target $0.01 to $0.05
- **Watch-through rate (WTR):** 25 percent or higher on a 30-second ad is strong
- **Cost per link click (CPC):** target $0.50 to $2.00
- **Cost per lead (CPL):** set a target based on your average customer value

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you run Meta video ads on a small budget?

Yes. Meta has no minimum ad spend requirement. A budget of $10 to $20 per day is enough to test a video ad and collect early data. Most agencies recommend at least $300 per month in total ad spend to gather enough impressions to make confident optimization decisions within a reasonable timeframe.

### Should you run ads on Facebook, Instagram, or both?

For most small businesses in 2026, running on both Facebook and Instagram through the Advantage Plus Placements option lets Meta automatically allocate budget to whichever placement performs better. Start with both placements combined, then split test once you have enough baseline data.

### How long does it take to see results from Meta video ads?

Most campaigns need two to four weeks to exit the learning phase, during which Meta gathers data on which users respond to your ad. Plan for a 30 to 60 day evaluation window before making major changes. Pausing and restarting an ad resets the algorithm's learning, which wastes budget and delays results.

### What is the difference between a boosted post and a proper Meta video ad?

A boosted post is a simplified promotion of an existing Facebook or Instagram post. A proper Meta video ad is built in Ads Manager with full control over audience, bid strategy, placement, and conversion pixel tracking. For any business goal beyond basic awareness, Ads Manager campaigns consistently outperform boosted posts.

### Do Meta video ads work for service businesses on Long Island?

Yes. Service businesses in Nassau County, Suffolk County and across Long Island are strong candidates for local Meta video ads because you can target by exact zip code or a radius around your location. Industries that see strong returns include home services, professional services, health and wellness, and food and restaurant.
