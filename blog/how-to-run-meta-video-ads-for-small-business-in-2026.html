How to Run Meta Video Ads for Small Business in 2026

Meta video ads cost $0.01 to $0.05 per view. This guide covers format, starting budget, audience setup, and four key metrics to track in 2026.

Kursad Yonet
October 27, 2026

Meta video ads on Facebook and Instagram typically cost $0.01 to $0.05 per view, making them the most accessible paid video channel for small businesses in 2026. You can start a test campaign for $10 to $20 per day, reach a precisely defined local or national audience, and measure whether people are watching and clicking. No other paid platform gives a local business this level of targeting control at this price point.

Quick Summary

  • Meta video ads typically cost $0.01 to $0.05 per view on Facebook and Instagram combined.
  • A useful starting test budget for a small business is $300 to $600 per month.
  • The best-performing formats are 15 to 30 second in-feed videos and 6 to 15 second Stories or Reels ads.
  • Facebook works better for audiences aged 35 and older; Instagram Reels performs strongest for 18 to 34.
  • The four metrics that matter are cost per view, watch-through rate, cost per link click, and cost per lead.
  • Mystudionet Productions delivers Meta-ready video ads in all required formats and aspect ratios, typically in 1 to 3 weeks.

What type of Meta video ad should a small business run?

For most small businesses in 2026, two formats generate the best results: in-feed video ads (15 to 30 seconds) and Stories or Reels ads (6 to 15 seconds). In-feed ads appear in the Facebook and Instagram scroll and give you room to tell a short story. Stories and Reels ads are full-screen and skippable, so the first two seconds have to stop the scroll cold.

The right choice depends on your goal. If you are building brand awareness or explaining a service that needs context, in-feed is the better fit. If you want fast conversions from a warm audience already familiar with your brand, Stories and Reels work faster.

For any format, the video must be 9:16 vertical for Stories and Reels, and 1:1 square or 16:9 horizontal for in-feed. A production partner that delivers all three aspect ratios from one production saves you from running separate shoots. How much does it cost to run Meta video ads for a small business?

Meta video ads typically cost between $0.01 and $0.05 per view and $0.50 to $2.00 per link click for most small business campaigns in 2026. Your total spend is controlled entirely by your daily or lifetime budget, so there is no minimum commitment and no contract.

A realistic budget framework for a local business:

  • Test phase (weeks 1 to 2): $10 to $20 per day to gather audience and creative data before scaling
  • Growth phase (months 2 to 3): $30 to $60 per day for ad sets showing a positive return
  • Ongoing campaigns: $300 to $1,200 per month total ad spend depending on goals and market size

The production cost of the video itself is separate from the ad spend. A professionally produced AI video ad typically runs from the low four figures to the mid four figures, and you amortize that cost across months of ad spend. For a full breakdown of production pricing, see how much an AI commercial costs in 2026.

The most common waste is running an underperforming creative too long. If your cost per link click exceeds $3.00 after 3,000 impressions, pause the ad, test a new hook, and re-run.

Strong creative is the variable that separates a profitable Meta campaign from one that bleeds budget.

How do you set up Meta video ad targeting for a local business?

Meta's audience targeting is the platform's biggest advantage over any other paid video channel. For most small businesses, start with one of three audience types:

  • Location targeting: set a radius around your town or county. For a Long Island business serving Nassau County or Suffolk County, a 15 to 25 mile radius with a broad age range gives the algorithm enough reach to find buyers without exhausting a tiny pool.
  • Interest and behavior audiences: target people who follow competitors, visit certain types of websites, or engage with content related to your product category.
  • Custom audiences and lookalikes: upload your customer email list and Meta builds a lookalike audience of people who are statistically similar to your best customers.

Retargeting is where Meta ads pay off fastest. Run a separate ad set targeting people who have visited your website or watched 50 percent of a previous video. These people already know your brand and convert at a much lower cost per lead than cold audiences.

Avoid making your initial audience too narrow. A local audience under 50,000 people will exhaust quickly and drive up costs. Start broad and let the algorithm learn which subset responds, then narrow from there.

What should a Meta video ad say?

A Meta video ad that converts follows a tight three-part structure: a scroll-stopping visual or verbal hook in the first 2 seconds, a specific problem or benefit in seconds 3 to 15, and one clear call to action at the end. The hook must work with the sound off because most Facebook and Instagram videos autoplay muted.

For small businesses, the most effective hooks are a before and after visual contrast, a specific number or result, or a direct statement of the viewer's problem. The rest of the ad should not try to say everything. Pick one message, one audience, one call to action.

How do you know if your Meta video ads are working?

Track four numbers, nothing else, in the first 60 days:

  • Cost per view (CPV): target $0.01 to $0.05. Above $0.10 means the creative is losing the auction.
  • Watch-through rate (WTR): percentage who watch to 50 percent or more. A WTR of 25 percent or higher on a 30-second ad is a strong signal.
  • Cost per link click (CPC): target $0.50 to $2.00 for most service businesses. Above $4.00 signals a need to test new creative or audience.
  • Cost per lead (CPL): set a target based on your average customer value. For a service business with a $2,000 average ticket, a CPL under $80 is typically profitable.

Do not optimize based on reach or impressions alone. Meta's algorithm prioritizes distribution volume by default. You want to optimize for the action that matters to your business, whether that is a form fill, a call, or a page visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you run Meta video ads on a small budget?

Yes. Meta has no minimum ad spend requirement. A budget of $10 to $20 per day is enough to test a video ad and collect early data. Most agencies recommend at least $300 per month in total ad spend to gather enough impressions to make confident optimization decisions within a reasonable timeframe.

Should you run ads on Facebook, Instagram, or both?

For most small businesses in 2026, running on both Facebook and Instagram through the Advantage Plus Placements option lets Meta automatically allocate budget to whichever placement performs better. Start with both placements combined, then split test once you have enough baseline data to make the comparison meaningful.

How long does it take to see results from Meta video ads?

Most campaigns need two to four weeks to exit the learning phase, during which Meta gathers data on which users respond to your ad. Plan for a 30 to 60 day evaluation window before making major changes. Pausing and restarting an ad resets the algorithm's learning, which wastes budget and delays results.

What is the difference between a boosted post and a proper Meta video ad?

A boosted post is a simplified promotion of an existing Facebook or Instagram post. A proper Meta video ad is built in Ads Manager with full control over audience, bid strategy, placement, and conversion pixel tracking. For any business goal beyond basic awareness, Ads Manager campaigns consistently outperform boosted posts because they give you precise targeting, A/B testing, and conversion optimization that boosting cannot match.

Do Meta video ads work for service businesses on Long Island?

Yes. Service businesses in Nassau County, Suffolk County and across Long Island are strong candidates for local Meta video ads because you can target by exact zip code or a radius around your location. Industries that consistently see positive returns include home services, professional services, health and wellness, and food and restaurant.
