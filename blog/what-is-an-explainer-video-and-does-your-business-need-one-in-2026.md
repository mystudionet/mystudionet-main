## What exactly is an explainer video?

An explainer video is a focused, concise video designed to explain a product, service, or process to a new audience. Unlike a brand film, which tells your company story, an explainer zeroes in on how something works and what problem it solves.

The three most common formats are:

- **Animated:** motion graphics, illustrated characters, or screen-recorded UI walkthroughs
- **Live-action:** a presenter, a product demo, or a real-world scenario
- **AI-generated:** photorealistic scenes produced with AI tools, combining the visual richness of live-action with the speed and flexibility of animation

Most effective explainers share the same structure: open with the customer's problem, introduce the solution, show how it works, and end with one clear call to action.

## How long should an explainer video be?

The sweet spot is 60 to 90 seconds. Engagement drops sharply after 90 seconds for explainer-style content. For complex products, a two-part approach works: a 60-second overview for the top of the funnel and a longer 2 to 3 minute deep-dive for engaged prospects.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an explainer video and a commercial?

A commercial is designed to create desire and drive immediate action, usually in 15 to 30 seconds, and is primarily a paid advertising tool. An explainer video is designed to educate and reduce buyer confusion, typically runs 60 to 90 seconds, and lives on a website, in sales emails, and at the top of the funnel. The two serve different purposes, though a strong commercial can include a brief explanatory moment.

### Can an explainer video be used as a paid ad?

Yes. A 60 to 90 second explainer can be cut into a 15 to 30 second ad for Meta, YouTube, or LinkedIn without a separate production. Most businesses that commission an explainer video ask for an ad cut at the same time to maximize the production investment.

### What makes an explainer video fail?

The two most common failure modes are trying to explain too much and starting with the company instead of the customer's problem. An effective explainer opens by naming the viewer's pain, not by introducing the brand. Poor audio is the second failure mode: a great visual with muddy or robotic voiceover loses the viewer immediately.
