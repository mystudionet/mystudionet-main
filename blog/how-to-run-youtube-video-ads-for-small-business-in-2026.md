## Do YouTube video ads actually work for small businesses?

Yes, YouTube video ads work for small businesses when the creative is strong and the targeting is specific. The platform gives small advertisers access to the same audience tools used by national brands: demographic filters, keyword intent from Google Search history, in-market audiences (people actively researching a category), and remarketing lists.

The most common failure is running a weak ad to a good audience. A compelling 30-second video that delivers one clear message will outperform a polished 90-second video that tries to say everything.

## What types of YouTube video ads should I use?

**Skippable in-stream ads** play before or during another video. Viewers can skip after 5 seconds. You only pay when someone watches 30 seconds or the full ad, or clicks through to your site. This is the best default format for most small business campaigns.

**Non-skippable in-stream ads** are 15 to 20 seconds and play through without a skip option. These work well for brand awareness messages.

**Bumper ads** run 6 seconds and are non-skippable. They work best as retargeting reminders after someone has already seen a longer ad or visited your website.

## How do I write a YouTube video ad script that converts?

The structure that works on YouTube mirrors what works on Meta and TikTok, with one key difference: the first 5 seconds are your only guaranteed window.

A reliable script structure for a 30-second skippable ad:

- **Seconds 0 to 5:** Hook. Name the viewer's problem or desire directly.
- **Seconds 5 to 20:** Solution and proof. Introduce what you do and give one concrete reason to believe it works.
- **Seconds 20 to 30:** Call to action. Tell the viewer exactly what to do next.

Keep the call to action singular. Asking viewers to both visit your website and follow your channel in the same ad typically reduces both actions.

## How much does YouTube video advertising cost in 2026?

Working industry ranges for 2026:

- **Cost per view (CPV):** $0.03 to $0.15 for skippable in-stream ads
- **Cost per thousand impressions (CPM):** $5 to $30 for bumper and non-skippable formats
- **Minimum starting budget:** $20 to $30 per day is a realistic test floor

A monthly test budget of $500 to $1,500 gives you enough data to identify which ad versions are working and which audiences respond best.

## Which metrics should I track for YouTube video ads?

Focus on four numbers:

- **View rate:** the percentage of viewers who watched 30 seconds or more. Above 30 percent indicates a strong hook.
- **Click-through rate (CTR):** industry average is 0.3 to 0.5 percent; above 1 percent is strong.
- **Cost per view (CPV):** track weekly to see if it trends down as the algorithm learns your audience.
- **Conversion rate:** how many viewers completed the defined action.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a YouTube video ad be?

For skippable in-stream ads, 15 to 45 seconds is the practical range. Ads between 15 and 30 seconds tend to achieve the highest view rates. Bumper ads are exactly 6 seconds. Non-skippable in-stream ads run 15 to 20 seconds.

### Can I run YouTube ads without a large production budget?

### Do I need a Google Ads account to run YouTube video ads?

Yes. YouTube advertising runs through Google Ads. You link your YouTube channel to a Google Ads account, upload your video, set your campaign type to "Video," choose your audience and budget, and launch.

### What is a good view rate for YouTube video ads?

A view rate above 30 percent for skippable in-stream ads is generally considered strong. Industry averages fall between 20 and 35 percent depending on the category and audience. Below 20 percent means the first 5 seconds are not connecting.

### Can YouTube ads work for local businesses on Long Island?

Yes. Google Ads allows geographic targeting down to a radius around a specific address, a ZIP code, a city, or a county. Businesses in Nassau County, Suffolk County, and across Long Island can focus their media spend on viewers within a specific radius of their location.
